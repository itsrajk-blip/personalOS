Part 1

Product requirements

Problem

Personal admin is scattered across notes apps, Gmail, reminders and memory. Three things go wrong as a result:

  • Records can't be pulled together when needed. At the vet or the GP, the symptoms noted over the past few weeks are spread across several apps.
  • Promises from other people get forgotten. The insurer said "within two weeks". Three weeks later nobody has chased it.
  • Deadlines live in photos and emails. An invoice photo or a renewal email has a due date that never makes it into a reminder.

Goals for v1

  1. Capture in under 10 seconds by text, voice or photo, with no filing decisions.
  2. Brief me: a one-page summary for any person or thing in your life (Joey's health, the HR-V) in under 30 seconds.
  3. Nothing slips: every deadline and every "we'll get back to you" shows on Today until it's done.
  4. Gmail without effort: relevant emails are picked up automatically and turned into items and follow-ups.
  5. Cheap to run: AI spend under $5 a month, with a hard cap at $10.

Not in v1

  • Other users, including your wife's own login. The data model will allow it later.
  • Email digest, a dedicated voice record button, photo gallery import.
  • Writing or sending email. Gmail stays strictly read-only.
  • Detecting replies automatically to close follow-ups. You mark them done.
  • Medical advice. The app records and summarises. It never diagnoses.
  • Billing, a public sign-up page, Google verification.

Who and what it tracks

Every item is tagged on two separate dimensions. Combining them is what makes "Joey + Health" a precise query. The AI can suggest a new entry (say, a second car), and nothing is added until you approve it.

The original text, photo or email snippet is always kept next to the extracted fields. Every tag can be corrected with one tap.

Follow-ups: the core object

On me

Something you have to do by a date. "Pay invoice by 24 Oct." "Call insurer on Monday."

Waiting on them

Someone promised to get back to you. "We'll respond within 10 business days." The app works out the expected date. Once that date passes, it moves to the top of Today as overdue, with a suggestion to chase.

Each follow-up can be Done (one tap) or Snoozed (tomorrow, next week, or a chosen date).

Today screen rules

  1. Overdue: past due, or waiting-on-them past the expected date. Red.
  2. Due today. Amber.
  3. New from Gmail: items found since you last looked. Keep or dismiss.
  4. Coming up: the next 7 days.
  5. Waiting on: open promises from others that aren't due yet.

Notifications

  • 7:00am Melbourne time, daily: "2 due today, 1 overdue." Skipped if there's nothing to report.
  • When Gmail finds something due within 3 days.
  • No other notifications. Push is free. It needs the app saved to your home screen and notifications allowed once.

Ask and briefs

You type or dictate a question, such as "brief me for Joey's vet visit" or "when did I last have a headache?". The app filters the database, then writes an answer from only the matching items. Every point links to the note or email it came from, so you can check it. If nothing matches, it says so rather than guessing.

Cost guardrails
  • The AI reads each item once. Browsing Today and timelines never calls the AI.
  • Free filters run before any AI: Gmail's Promotions and Social tabs and emails with an unsubscribe header are skipped. Quoted replies and signatures are stripped.
  • Fixed instructions are cached between calls, which cuts repeat input cost.
  • A hard $10 monthly limit set in the Anthropic console, plus month-to-date spend shown in Settings.
  • Expected: $1 to $3 a month after the backfill.

Privacy and security

  • Sign-in with Google, locked to one email address. Anyone else is refused.
  • Database rules: every row is readable only by its owner. Photos sit in private storage behind expiring links.
  • Gmail permission is gmail.readonly. The app cannot send, delete or change email. The access token is stored encrypted.
  • Only the extracted signal and a short snippet are stored from email, with a link back to the original in Gmail. Full emails are not copied.
  • Data is sent only to Anthropic for processing. Anthropic doesn't train on API data by default.
  • Export everything as a file, or delete everything, from Settings.

Retrieval: why there are no vectors

Every item is tagged when it comes in, so most questions are an exact filter on tags. Postgres keyword search over the original text catches anything the tags missed. At a few thousand items this is faster, cheaper and more precise than embeddings. If Ask starts missing things, embeddings can be added later without changing anything else.

Gmail sync

  • Connect once through Google's consent screen. The OAuth app is set to "In production" without verification, so access doesn't expire after 7 days. You'll click past an "unverified app" warning once.
  • Backfill: the last 90 days, filtered, sent through the Anthropic Batch API overnight.
  • Ongoing: every 15 minutes, Gmail's change history returns only new messages since the last check. Cheap and fast.
  • Free filters first: skip Promotions and Social, skip emails with an unsubscribe header, skip anything already seen. Strip quoted replies, signatures and HTML.
  • Stored: the extracted signal, a 300-character snippet, the sender, the date and a link to open the email in Gmail.

Part 3

UI and UX design

Five tabs. Today is the home screen. Capture is the big button in the middle, always one tap away. All content in these mockups is sample data.

Design principles

  • Capture first, file never. You never pick a folder or tag. You only correct one when it's wrong.
  • State at a glance. Colour stripe and label on every follow-up: red overdue, amber today, blue-grey waiting, indigo new.
  • Every AI output shows its source. Briefs cite notes. Gmail finds show the sender and snippet.
  • Thumb reach. Main actions sit in the bottom half of the screen.
